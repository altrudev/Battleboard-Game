# Core Game Loop

## Player premise

The player wants to enter the Grand Battleboard Tournament but cannot register without an eligible board. The opening progression is therefore about finding, evaluating, recruiting, training, and assigning fighters until the player can field a qualified board.

## Primary loop

1. Discover recruit candidates through the world, local circuits, scouting, relationships, challenges, and story encounters.
2. Scout incomplete information about each recruit: combat traits, temperament, experience, board aptitude, predispositions, and growth potential.
3. Recruit or negotiate with candidates. Some recruits may require trust, a challenge, a contract, a story objective, or another prerequisite.
4. Train fighters in combat skills and one or more board-position disciplines.
5. Observe affinities, conflicts, shared experiences, and emerging relationships through training and matches.
6. Assign fighters to Pawn, Knight, Bishop, Rook, Queen, or King positions according to aptitude, current form, opponent, and board chemistry.
7. Select a starting board plus reserves from a larger roster.
8. Enter qualification matches where tactical board movement transitions seamlessly into direct third-person encounters.
9. Resolve injuries, experience, relationship changes, reputation, scouting knowledge, promotions, and new recruitment opportunities.
10. Improve the organization and repeat until tournament eligibility is achieved.

## Recruit model

A fighter is not a fixed chess class. Each recruit has a multi-position aptitude profile and may train or certify for several positions.

Each recruit includes:
- identity and background
- current combat capability
- growth potential
- position aptitudes
- learned position mastery
- personality and temperament
- predispositions
- relationship graph
- shared experience tags
- training history
- skills and equipment
- injuries and scars
- current form and morale
- career history

## Affinity and predisposition

Predisposition describes the kinds of fighters, backgrounds, experiences, tactical philosophies, and formations a recruit naturally performs well or poorly around.

Relationship affinity is learned through actual shared history and can eventually outweigh initial predisposition.

Board geometry activates only the relationships and support links that are relevant to the current formation. Affinity should produce visible gameplay events where possible rather than remaining a hidden numerical bonus.

Examples include:
- street-experienced fighters forming a Wolfpack resonance
- mentor/student support
- disciplined fighters stabilizing volatile recruits
- rivals gaining a Vengeance response after one is defeated
- a Lone Wolf becoming stronger when unsupported
- shared-survivor experience improving resolve under critical health

## Board construction

The player eventually owns more fighters than can be deployed in one match. Roster construction therefore matters as much as individual fighter strength.

A stronger fighter is not automatically the best assignment. The better choice may be a lower-rated fighter whose position aptitude, opponent matchup, relationships, predisposition, or support geometry improves the entire board.

## Progression target

The first major campaign objective is not winning the championship. It is becoming eligible to enter it.

Eligibility can require:
- a registered Boardmaster
- required position coverage
- a minimum roster depth
- minimum board rating
- qualification victories
- valid fighter certifications
- tournament licence level

The full tournament begins only after the player has built a legitimate board and learned how the organization functions.
